## Design Context

### Users

**Primary:** Dialysis patients in Thailand — often elderly, frequently fatigued or unwell, using the app to manage a serious chronic condition. They open the app before a dialysis session, during a post-session recovery period, or when anxious about nutrition or scheduling. Cognitive load is a real constraint: they may be tired, on medication, or emotionally stressed.

**Secondary:** Professional Care Buddy caregivers — escort patients to dialysis sessions, manage their job schedule, and track earnings. More tech-comfortable than patients, but still need clear, efficient interfaces.

**Job to be done:**
- Patients: Find a trusted caregiver, book a session, know exactly what to eat, and feel that everything is under control.
- Caregivers: See available jobs, accept and track an active session, know when and how much they get paid.

---

### Brand Personality

**Three words:** Clinical · Efficient · Modern

The product is a professional medical service, not a wellness app. Patients trust it because it behaves like real healthcare software — structured, precise, and dependable. It should feel like it was built by people who understand dialysis, not by a startup trying to make healthcare feel "fun."

**Emotional goals:** Confidence (everything is organized), calm (nothing alarming or noisy), and dignity (patients are not made to feel like their condition is a burden).

---

### Aesthetic Direction

**Theme:** Light mode only. Dialysis patients often use the app in hospital waiting rooms, in bright daylight, or on older devices. Dark mode is an anti-reference.

**Visual tone:** Modern clinical — the intersection of Apple Health's spatial clarity and the structured dependability of Thai government/banking apps that Thai users already trust. Not Western hospital grey (no Epic/Cerner palette), not startup health-tech (no purple gradient meditation vibes), not dark fintech.

**Color approach:** The existing M3 token system uses `#255db0` (primary blue), `#685783` (tertiary purple), and a near-white `#faf9fe` background. This is a solid foundation — the blue reads as modern and precise without being institutional. Accents should be used sparingly; white space and type hierarchy do the visual work.

**Typography:** Manrope (headlines) + Lexend (body/labels) — already established. Lexend was specifically designed for reading speed and accessibility, ideal for elderly Thai patients. **Run type sizes larger than typical web apps** — base body at 17–18px rather than 16px. Heading scales should have strong contrast ratios between steps.

**Anti-references:**
- Generic hospital/EMR blue (Epic, Cerner, government health portal look)
- Startup health-tech gradient aesthetic (purple-to-teal, glowing cards, Calm/Headspace vibes)
- Dark mode / high-tech / fintech aesthetics
- Childish or cartoon health vibes

---

### Design Principles

1. **Clarity over decoration** — Every visual element must reduce friction, not add interest. Empty space is always preferable to decorative noise. No sparklines, no gradient overlays on cards, no animated counters.

2. **Medical precision** — Layouts are structured and predictable. Important information (next appointment, medication alerts, lab values) is immediately visible without scrolling. Never bury critical data below the fold.

3. **Dignified scale** — Type defaults run large. Thai elderly patients are the primary user; 18px body, generous line-height, strong label contrast. Tap targets are at minimum 48px.

4. **Thai-contextual professionalism** — Not cold Western hospital grey. The warmth of Thai healthcare culture — respectful, personal, attentive — expressed through modern typographic precision and thoughtful spacing, not through playful colors or mascots.

5. **Calm authority** — The primary blue is used purposefully: actions, links, key data points. White surfaces and generous padding dominate. Never plaster the primary color across decorative backgrounds or icon grids.
